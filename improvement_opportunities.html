5 Key Opportunities for OHAC Growth

Synthesized from member feedback on improvements and desired activities

This analysis draws together themes from two open-ended survey questions: "What parts of the club could be improved and what could make it stronger?" and "What types of club activity would add the most value for you?" The opportunities below reflect the most frequently mentioned and high-impact areas for club development.

1
Most Requested

Expand Social Running and Community Events

Post-run socializing emerged as the single most-requested activity, with members across all membership types citing desire for post-run coffee/food gatherings as a key value-add. This reflects strong appetite for community-building beyond the run itself.

Specific opportunities:
  • Post-run gatherings: Regular coffee/food events after Saturday runs and Tuesday sessions
  • Social events: Quiz nights, seasonal celebrations, member recognition events
  • Weekend options: Sunday long runs (10 mentions) and Saturday morning alternatives to current afternoon focus
  • Walking group: Low-barrier entry for newer or social members

Member demand: Post-run coffee/food mentioned by 11 respondents; Sunday long run by 10; quiz nights by 5+

2
Communication Foundation

Overhaul Digital Presence and Information Architecture

Website and communication consistency were cited as barriers to engagement and barriers to access of key information. Multiple members noted outdated website content, unclear information hierarchy, and confusion about which channel to use for which updates.

Specific opportunities:
  • Website redesign: Modern layout with prioritized essential info, updated content, clear navigation for new members
  • Communication strategy: Define role and audience for each channel (public Facebook, private group, website, email, calendar)
  • Information clarity: Centralized, consistent format for upcoming runs, pace info, who to contact, what activities are available
  • Multi-channel approach: Ensure information reaches members via preferred channels (email, Facebook, website, SMS/WhatsApp)

Member feedback: Website noted as "out of date," "needs overhaul," and "hard to find information" across 5+ responses. Private Facebook group rated most useful (15/37 members), suggesting strong preference for centralized platforms.

3
Structural Development

Build Coaching and Training Programs

Coaching options for seniors, structured training programs, and knowledge-sharing sessions were repeatedly cited as valuable additions. This reflects demand for club-led skill development across age groups.

Specific opportunities:
  • Senior coaching: Dedicated coaching for older/experienced members (mentioned separately from youth coaching)
  • Race-specific training: Targeted programs (8 mentions) aligned to key club races and championships
  • SME sessions: Expert talks on training, nutrition, injury prevention, technique
  • Structured progression: Clear pathways for beginners to intermediate to competitive levels
  • Track/athletics training: Formal access to technical coaching (high jump, long jump, throws, sprints, hurdles)

Member demand: Coaching options mentioned in 6+ responses; race-specific training by 8; access to athletics coaching by 4

4
Inclusivity & Belonging

Bridge Junior/Senior Divide and Build Inclusive Community

Members highlighted that junior and senior activities, despite existing, can feel disconnected. There is appetite for cross-generational mentorship, reduced gatekeeping of informal groups, and greater visibility of volunteer opportunities.

Specific opportunities:
  • Family integration: Activities and communication that bring juniors, parents, and seniors together
  • Volunteer engagement: Clear pathways to get involved; recognition of volunteer workload
  • Women-focused activities: Women's social runs, senior women recruitment, dedicated facilities (e.g., shower access)
  • Beginner runs: Low-pace, low-pressure group runs to attract and retain new members
  • Transparency on membership: Clear communication about benefits, discounts, who is eligible for what

Member feedback: Junior/senior disconnect noted by 3+ members; volunteer workload mentioned by parents; more women-focused activities requested; gatekeeping of informal groups noted

5
Operational Scalability

Standardize Pace Groups and Differentiated Run Options

Multiple members mentioned confusion about pace, desire for clearly-labeled pace groups, and preference for knowing run details in advance (pace, distance, route). This reflects need for more structure and transparency in the run program.

Specific opportunities:
  • Clear pace labeling: Standardized pace categories (e.g., beginner, easy, moderate, fast) across all runs
  • Diverse run formats: Maintain range of speeds and distances (some members want "easy at 5:20," others want faster options)
  • Named run leaders: Consistent leaders/captains for each run to provide continuity and approachability
  • Pre-run information: Publish location, distance, elevation, expected finish time, pace before each run
  • Different locations: Rotate runs across Clubrooms (Northern Suburbs), City/waterfront, and other accessible venues

Member demand: Different pace groups mentioned by 8+; knowing pace beforehand by 3+; named run leaders by 2+; location variety by 4+

Next Steps

These five opportunities represent the highest-impact areas to focus on in the next 6–12 months. They are drawn from direct member feedback and address barriers to participation, unmet needs, and requests for greater community connection.

Quick wins: Post-run gatherings, pace group clarity, and communication clarification can be implemented with minimal resources.

Longer-term: Website overhaul, coaching program formalization, and junior/senior integration may require more planning and volunteer coordination.

Methodology: Analysis synthesized two open-ended survey questions from 37 OHAC members (survey period 2026-08-09 to 2026-08-15). Multi-select and free-text responses were coded for recurring themes, aggregated by frequency, and prioritized by member impact and implementation feasibility.

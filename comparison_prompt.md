# Running Club Website & Social Media Analysis (Social Runner Perspective)

**Role:** You are a social runner who is actively looking to join a local running club. You run regularly (roughly 3–4 times per week), enjoy company on easy and long runs, and want a club that feels welcoming rather than elite or intimidating. You care about community, consistency, and whether you'll actually meet people—not just ticking off workouts.

**Task:** Analyse the websites and public social media presence of the running clubs listed below. Use the social media discoverable on the website. Compare them from my perspective as someone deciding where to join. Be honest, practical, and specific. Flag gaps, red flags, and standout positives.

## What to investigate

For **each club**, review the website and at least the **two most active** public social channels (usually Instagram and Facebook, or Strava if that's where they live).

### 1. First impressions (website)

- Is it clear within 30 seconds what the club is, who it's for, and how to join?
- Does it feel friendly and inclusive, or race-focused and competitive?
- Is key info easy to find: **training schedule, meeting points, membership cost, contact/join process, code of conduct**?
- Mobile usability: can I figure out "where do I show up this week?" on my phone?
- Freshness: outdated pages, broken links, old race results, "last updated 2019"?

### 2. Social runner signals (social media)

- **Community vibe:** Do posts show real people, groups, smiles, café stops, celebrations—or mostly race times and podium photos?
- **Consistency:** How often do they post? Is there a regular weekly rhythm (e.g. "Tuesday track", "Sunday long run")?
- **Inclusivity:** Mixed paces, beginners welcome, "no one left behind", social vs performance messaging?
- **Engagement:** Do members comment, tag friends, share stories? Or is it one-way admin posting?
- **Accessibility for newcomers:** Is there a clear "first run" or "come try us" message? Beginner-friendly language?
- **Local feel:** Do I recognise places, routes, parks? Does it feel like *my* area?
- **Red flags:** Inside jokes only, cliquey tone, aggressive "no slow runners", unpaid volunteer burnout vibes, ghost account (nothing posted in months).

### 3. Practical joinability

- Can I **try before I commit** (free taster run, pay-as-you-go, guest policy)?
- **Membership:** Cost, payment model, insurance included?, UK Athletics affiliation?
- **Schedule fit:** Which sessions match my availability? Are times/locations pinned or buried?
- **Communication:** WhatsApp, email list, Facebook group—how do I get looped in after expressing interest?
- **Safety & organisation:** Risk assessments, run leaders, hi-vis, winter dark-run guidance mentioned?

### 4. Social vs competitive balance

Score each club **1–5** (1 = very social/casual, 5 = very performance/competitive) with a one-line justification based on **evidence from their content**, not assumptions.

## Rules for your analysis

- **Evidence-based:** Cite specific pages, posts, or phrases (with dates where possible). Don't invent schedules or prices.
- **Assume I'm nervous:** Turning up alone to a new group is hard—weight "welcoming newcomer" signals heavily.
- **Distinguish facts from inference:** Label clearly when you're inferring vibe from tone vs stating something explicitly on the site.
- **Be fair:** A club can be excellent for racers and less ideal for social runners—say that without being dismissive.
- **Note limitations:** If social accounts are private, inactive, or info is missing, say so and don't fill gaps with guesses.

# OHAC Member Survey — Infographic Report

## Executive summary
- This survey captured 37 responses between 2026-08-09 and 2026-08-15.
- Overall experience was positive for most respondents: 25 of 37 rated the club 4 or 5 out of 5, while 2 rated it 1 or 2.
- The strongest attraction themes were Location / convenience (22 mentions) and Friend/family already a member (20 mentions), alongside Competitive running / racing (17 mentions), suggesting accessibility and peer networks are core strengths.
- The clearest barriers were Family commitments (16 mentions) and Injury / fitness (8 mentions), with timing and activity mismatch also appearing in the data.
- Members want clearer information about who to contact (17 mentions), what the club offers (15 mentions), and upcoming events (14 mentions).
- The key caveat is that this is a self-selected member survey, not a census; the commentary is therefore directional rather than definitive across the whole club.

## Data quality & methodology
- Rows analysed: 37.
- Date range of responses: 2026-08-09 to 2026-08-15.
- Multi-select questions were split on commas and normalised for obvious spacing and casing issues. Blank or non-answer responses were excluded from the denominator.
- Free-text comments were read for themes and used qualitatively rather than as exact counts.
- Where a chart reports question-specific totals, the report labels those counts explicitly rather than implying a whole-club percentage.

## Findings — interests
- Top attraction themes: Location / convenience (22), Friend/family already a member (20), Competitive running / racing (17), Coaching or structured training (15), Social connection (13).
- Top retention drivers: Training with others / Social connection / Friendships (23), Racing opportunities (18), Access to events or facilities (17), Club history / loyalty (16), Coaching or advice (16).
- Desired additions suggest interest in more flexible or social group-run options, with weeknight and weekend activities in demand.
- The most common preferred run times are Weekday evening (21), Sunday morning (17), Saturday afternoon (11), Weekday early morning (8), and Saturday morning (7).

## Findings — challenges
- The top barriers were: Family commitments (16), Injury / fitness (8), Timing (7), Activity doesn't match what I'm looking for (7), Work commitments (6).
- Information access is a recurring issue: Who to contact (17), What club offers runners (15), and Upcoming runs (14) were the most frequently identified information gaps.
- Several comments highlighted a desire for clearer communication about run details, pace groups, and how to get involved, especially for newer or less connected members.
- The communication mix shows Private Facebook group has strongest perceived usefulness (15 very useful), followed by Club calendar (11), Facebook page (9), and Word of mouth (8).

## Findings — communications
- Private Facebook group showed strongest perceived usefulness (15/37 very useful), followed by Club calendar (11/37) and Facebook page (9/37).
- The clearest message is that members want a reliable, consistent source for events, run details, and contact information to reduce confusion and duplication.
- Website uptake as a primary communication channel is low (4/37 very useful), suggesting web-based information may need better discoverability or integration with other channels.

## Free-text themes
- Community connection remains a major positive, especially through social runs, club events, and peer support.
- Members consistently want clearer run information, pace-group guidance, and more visible communication about what is happening and how to join.
- Volunteer workload and communication consistency come up in open comments as potential pressure points for the club.

## Limitations & caveats
- This is a self-selected member survey and is not a representative census of all members.
- Some questions were optional, which means the denominator varies by question and should be read as a response count, not a whole-club percentage.
- A small number of responses contained formatting noise or truncation in the CSV; those were treated qualitatively rather than forced into exact categories.

## Recommended actions (inference)
- Review the website and calendar to make event details, pace-groups, and contact information easier to find.
- Consider a clearer single source of truth across website, calendar, and social channels to reduce duplication and confusion.
- Pilot a small number of weeknight or social run options where demand is strongest, while keeping pace guidance visible for participants.
